Nutrition and healthy eating in Australia

What Australians eat—and what shapes it

Thirteen visual sections trace the national diet from nutrients and age to food access, geography and the local food environment.

Read from top to bottom for the story. Each chart includes a highlighted finding; hover over marks for exact values.

01

The national diet

Energy totals hide what food is made of

Australians aged two years and over consumed an average of 8,114 kJ a day in 2023. The mix of nutrients—and the variation within food categories—reveals more than the total alone.

02

Age and time

Eating patterns move across the life course

Supplement use rises in later life. Sweetened-drink consumption follows a different pattern: it has fallen since 1995, but remains concentrated among adolescents and younger adults.

03

Guidelines and access

Recommendations meet unequal circumstances

Australians fall short across several core food groups. Income adds a more fundamental constraint: some households cannot reliably obtain enough food at all.

04

Place and food

Three map types connect diet and place

Colour compares state energy shares, graduated circles show nutrient intake, and a Melbourne case study highlights grocery and fresh-produce outlets. Read each legend: these maps use different measures and geographic scales.

Takeaway

A national average is only the beginning.

Australian eating patterns vary across age, time, income and place. Progress in sweetened-drink consumption sits beside persistent guideline shortfalls and unequal access to food.
